Let’s Talk about Digital You

Technical and Ethical Aspects of a Data-Centric World: a for-credit, interdisciplinary course open to students in any major.
My roleProject manager and lead learning experience designer
FormatHybrid, semester-long course in Canvas, Spring 2024
PartnersFaculty from the School of Medicine and Duke Law, plus guest experts
TimelineApril 2023 to a May 2024 evaluation
01 · The challenge

One course with many perspectives

Two lead instructors and a rotating guest each week. The design challenge was cohesion: one integrated course instead of disconnected mini-lectures.
Lead instructors2
Supplemental instructors2
Guest lecturers12
TAs10
Design team members5

03 · The design

Outcomes established a weekly rhythm

I defined three outcomes with the lead instructors, then mapped every week to the same five-step structure so guests and TAs could begin co-design work.
Outcome 1Interdisciplinary understandingSee how different disciplines approach the same problem.
Outcome 2Technical know-how and ethical reasoningPair hands-on understanding of a technology with reasoning about its effects.
Outcome 3Reflection on digital identityConnect each theme to students’ own digital lives.
A typical week · select a step
LectureA guest expert grounds about 100 students in the week’s technology or ethical framework, with live polls and think-pair-share.
SupportsInterdisciplinary understanding
Small-group labHands-on exploration in EdStem with TA support. Students could opt out on ethical grounds.
SupportsTechnical know-how
Case study discussionSmaller sections work through a case, with TA guides for sensitive conversations about AI and ethics.
SupportsEthical reasoning
ReflectionA weekly post on each student’s own WordPress site, shared in Canvas for peer response.
SupportsReflection on digital identity
Co-curricularTwo activities outside class from a provided list, like a speaker series, a club, or an op-ed.
SupportsAll three outcomes, beyond the classroom
04 · Flexibility and support

Built for choice and facilitated with intention

Universal Design for Learning gave students agency, and Duke-supported tools and facilitation guides kept the course consistent.
UDL · EngagementStudents chose co-curricular events and could opt out of labs.
UDL · RepresentationLectures, panels, readings, and captioned videos, with recordings in Canvas.
UDL · Action and expressionFinal projects as an essay, podcast, or video.
Tools
CanvasThe central hub, with consistent weekly navigation.
EdStemTechnical labs with in-class TA support, chosen after prototyping.
Sites@Duke ExpressStudent WordPress sites for weekly reflections.
Facilitation
Large lecturesWooClap polls for about 100 students, with instructor coaching.
TA guidesFor leading labs and sensitive AI and ethics conversations.
TemplatesKept the course cohesive across rotating guests.
05 · Project management

Course development timeline

A team charter, two tracking boards, and built-in QA kept faculty, designers, and media on schedule.

Nov–Dec 2023Onboarded TAs and coached instructors
Jan–May 2024Launched, gathered feedback, evaluated and debriefed
Project management essentials

Two tracking boardsExternal for faculty and leadership; internal for detailed tasks.

Iterative reviewEvery asset reviewed as created, plus a second reviewer.
