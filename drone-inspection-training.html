01 · Task analysis

What the job requires

I start by breaking the job into duties, tasks and steps, then rate each task by how much harm a mistake could cause. Every task becomes a performance objective with a condition, a behavior and a standard. Select a duty and a task to see how it breaks down.
About the numbersAircraft limits, clearance distances, battery reserves and image tolerances on this page are illustrative values for one aircraft configuration. They are not real-world operating standards. The task analysis and company limits would need validation with flight and utility safety experts. Operating rules throughout follow the FAA’s Part 107 regulations summary.

How practice builds toward the checkride
  1. 01DemonstrationAn instructor walks through the task and names each decision as it comes up.Feedback: trainees explain the steps back in their own words.
  2. 02Guided simulator practiceTrainees make the decisions with prompts and optional hints.Feedback: coaching after each choice, with a chance to try again.
  3. 03Independent scenarioThe same tasks under new conditions, with no hints.Feedback: an instructor debrief from the simulator record.
  4. 04Observed field flightTrainees fly the company’s aircraft at a practice site while an instructor watches.Feedback: rubric-based notes ahead of the checkride.
03 · Simulator scenario

Decisions under pressure

Scenario scripts put the critical tasks into situations where the right call isn’t obvious. Each decision point is tied to an evaluation criterion, so the simulator records what the trainee chose and the instructor can debrief from it. Support fades as it goes: the first decision includes a prompt, the next two offer an optional hint, and the last two are on your own. After coaching, you can try a decision again. The limits and tolerances are illustrative values. This is one sample scenario. Later scenarios would test the same tasks under different conditions, with no hints. Try the substation inspection.
SIM-OPS · 03Substation inspection
Notice this
Sample engineering requirement: the insulator centered in the frame guide so every disc is in view, exposure within ±0.3 EV so cracks and flashover marks aren’t lost to glare, and 25–35 ft out so the lens resolves the detail inspectors assess. Focus and resolution are confirmed in the field image review. Sample clearance: no closer than 20 ft, measured from the nearest point of the aircraft to the nearest energized conductor. If a view can’t be captured from outside the approved clearance, stop and log it for a reflight. If the plan gives no approved clearance, ask the flight lead.

04 · Practical evaluation

The field checkride

Company qualification depends on what the trainee, already holding a Remote Pilot Certificate, does with the company’s aircraft. This sample rubric is completed by a qualified evaluator, not the trainee. Each item has a behavior-based anchor describing what “met” looks like, and in use the evaluator records an observation note for each item as evidence. This screen demonstrates the qualification logic only. Missing any critical item means the trainee isn’t qualified yet, however well the rest went. Mark items as an evaluator would to see the result.

05 · Measuring effectiveness

What changes on the job

Measures are planned with the curriculum, so the data needed to revise it is collected from the first cohort.
During trainingSimulator decisions, attempts and hint use per scenario, planned for the LMS as xAPI statements (not built in this prototype)Informs: where trainees need several tries, I add a worked example or more guided practice to the lesson before it.
At qualificationRubric results by item, including first-attempt pass rate, compared with simulator results for the same tasksInforms: if trainees succeed in the simulator but miss the item in the field, the observed field practice gets more time.
On the jobFlight lead observations after qualification, incident and near-miss reports, and how often inspection data has to be re-flownInforms: a repeated pattern sends me back to the objective and standard behind it.
When an item is missed often, I trace it back through the course map to the lesson and objective behind it, and revise there first.
