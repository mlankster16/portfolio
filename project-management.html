03 · Agile

Agile

Work is planned and delivered in short sprints, with a working version reviewed at the end of each one.
↻ Repeats each sprint
↻ Repeats each sprint
Step 1 of 5Product & backlog

Each product starts with a short definition: what it is, who it’s for, the problem it solves, and what success looks like at MVP. The owner then builds a backlog of cards sorted into Discovery, Build, Test, Refine, Deploy, and Icebox.

Output: a product definition and a prioritized backlog on our Monday.com board

Step 2 of 5Sprint planning

At the start of each one-week sprint, each person pulls two to four ready cards from their backlog and writes a one-sentence sprint goal. The goal stays fixed for the week. The cards underneath it can be swapped, split, or added to.

Output: a sprint goal, committed cards, and estimated hours

Step 3 of 5Build & check in

Midway through the sprint, everyone posts a short async update: progress toward their goal, any blocked cards, and what support they need. Blockers come up here instead of waiting for the review.

Output: an async update in Teams, with blocked cards flagged on the board

Step 4 of 5Review & demo

At the end of each sprint, each person shows what they built or learned and reflects on whether they met their goal. Work can be rough; the point is to make progress visible to the team.

Output: goal met, partial, or not met, plus cards carried into the next sprint

Step 5 of 5Retrospective

Right after the review, a 15-minute conversation about how we worked rather than what we built: what went well, what was hard, and what we’ll change next sprint.

Output: one or two adjustments for the next sprint, and blockers escalated to leadership

04 · Hybrid

Hybrid

A fixed roadmap of phases and sign-offs, with scheduled cycles of review, feedback, and implementation inside it.
↻ Review, feedback, implement
↻ Review, feedback, implement
Step 1 of 5Kickoff & discovery

A project kickoff and onboarding for each faculty lead, followed by one-on-one walkthroughs with each member of the design team to discuss the course, its learning goals, and what it should include.

Milestone: onboarding and course walkthroughs complete, with faculty sign-off

Step 2 of 5Design & alignment

Faculty leads develop their course design with the design team through ongoing planning meetings. Full project team meetings bring every course together for alignment, and the gaps or open questions they surface shape the next co-design session.

Milestone: course design drafted, with faculty sign-off

Step 3 of 5Co-design

Practicing nurses respond to the questions and gaps identified during design. Faculty leads and the design team refine each course from that input before production begins.

Milestone: course and module maps finalized, and every learning asset identified

Step 4 of 5Production & build

Scripting, studio recording, video editing, and course build. Scripts and video edits each get a dedicated review round, and a second co-design session gathers feedback across all courses near completion.

Milestone: video edits approved and all courses built and ready for review

Step 5 of 5Review & launch

Continuing education review, stakeholder feedback, launch preparation, and submission to Coursera.

Milestone: courses launch-ready and the Specialization launched

05 · Traditional

Traditional (Waterfall)

Work moves through defined phases in order, with each phase reviewed and approved before the next begins.
Step 1 of 5Initiate

Meetings with faculty and leadership define the project’s scope, objectives, and success criteria, and identify everyone who has a stake in the outcome.

Milestone: project charter and stakeholder register approved

Step 2 of 5Plan

An integrated project plan sets the scope, schedule, and cost baselines, supported by a work breakdown structure, a communication plan, and a risk register.

Milestone: baselines approved, with milestones for drafts, media production, and launch

Step 3 of 5Develop

The team designs course materials, produces video, and builds assignments in sequence. Progress is tracked against the baselines, and any change goes through a formal change request.

Milestone: course materials, videos, and assignments complete

Step 4 of 5Review & QA

Courses are checked against accessibility and quality standards, then reviewed by stakeholders before launch.

Milestone: stakeholder acceptance of the courses

Step 5 of 5Launch & close

Courses launch, lessons learned are documented, project files are archived, and ownership moves to the team that maintains the courses.

Milestone: launch and handoff for ongoing maintenance
