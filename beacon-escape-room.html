02 · The standard

From standard to activity

The escape room is built on one Grade 6 Common Core standard. Below, I map the expectations this activity focuses on to essential questions and “I can” goals, with the rooms where learners practiced each one.
Common Core · Grade 6 · 6.RP.A.3 ↗Use ratio and rate reasoning to solve real-world and mathematical problems.This activity focuses on selected parts of the standard, mainly 3a (equivalent ratios and ratio tables) and 3b (unit rates). It doesn’t practice plotting ratio pairs from 3a, percents (3c), or unit conversions (3d). The final panel asks learners to use 3a and 3b together.

03 · The puzzles

How the puzzles build ratio reasoning

Grade 6 standard 6.RP.A.3 asks learners to use ratio and rate reasoning to solve real-world and mathematical problems. This activity focuses on selected parts of it, mainly 3a and 3b. Learners move from visual models to tables and rates, then use what they have learned to make a final decision.

04 · Design

A few choices behind the design

→4810612?→
Make the math visible.Learners build the lens ratio with tiles, extend a ratio table, and use a double number line to reason about the radio rate.
More ▾Each representation helps make the relationship clear before learners choose a plan.
1What stays the same?2Can you show it in a table?3What happens in 1 minute?
Offer support without adding pressure.Optional hints move from a guiding question to a more concrete next step.
More ▾Learners can try again without a timer or penalty, and feedback points them back to the part of the relationship they may want to reconsider.
Plan A works because…The radio sends 6 beeps a minute, so…
Make room for learners to explain.At the final panel, learners choose a plan and identify why it works.
More ▾In class, they can compare their reasoning and the representations they used. The activity can be completed independently first, then used to start a short discussion about how equivalent ratios and unit rates helped solve the problem.
