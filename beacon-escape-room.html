Light the Beacon

A virtual escape room for Grade 6 math, where ratio reasoning brings a storm-damaged lighthouse back online.
Play the escape room ↓
At a glance

I designed this for fun to explore how to make ratios and rates more exciting for 6th grade math students. This idea was inspired by conversations with my middle school students a few years ago. It stuck with me, and so here’s what I would build for them if I were still in the classroom. I designed the learning sequence, practice, and feedback, then built the playable prototype with Claude Design, Claude Code, and ChatGPT. It’s a self-paced browser activity for independent or classroom use.

About this prototype: the activity design is my own. The images are concept mockups I generated with ChatGPT for this proof of concept, and I built the interaction with Claude Design and Claude Code.

02 · Key decisions

Three design decisions to support learning

  1. Make the math visible.Learners build the lens ratio with tiles, extend a ratio table, and use a double number line to reason about the radio rate.
  2. Offer support without adding pressure.Every room offers an optional three-part practice and three hints that move from a guiding question to a concrete next step.
  3. Make room for learners to explain.In the signal gallery, learners set each dial from their logbook clues, test the signal, and pick the two reasons it works.
03 · Details

Want the details?

Room by room▾

Built on Common Core 6.RP.A.3 (opens in a new tab) ↗, mainly 3a (equivalent ratios and ratio tables) and 3b (unit rates). Each room pairs one part of the standard with a hands-on choice, an optional practice round, three hints, and feedback for each kind of mistake.

Feedback for a common mistake A learner answers 12 blue, 8 gold: So close, but the colors are switched. You have 3 blue for every 2 gold.
The taskFill a 20-spot lens so it keeps 2 blue for every 3 gold, counting the 4 gold panels already in place.
The math6.RP.A.3a · Equivalent ratios“I can build equivalent ratios and complete a ratio table.”How can two different-looking ratios describe the same relationship?
Hands-on choice · Describe itLearners finish “There are __ blue lights for every __ gold lights,” then build two groups in a practice lamp.
Practice firstDescribe a ratio with “for every,” read and extend groups, then build a 10-spot practice lamp.
Hints, from a nudge to a next step
  1. 1Hint 1: What is the ratio of blue to gold? How many lights are in one group of that ratio?
  2. 2Hint 2: The lens has 20 spots. How many groups of 5 is that?
  3. 3Hint 3: Four groups of 2 blue and 3 gold is how many of each? The 4 stuck gold panels count too.
Feedback for a common mistake A learner answers 14 flashes: Close. When the packs doubled from 4 to 8, the flashes doubled too. Did your answer grow the same way?
The taskComplete a ratio table: 4 packs make 6 flashes, so 10 packs make 15.
The math6.RP.A.3a · Equivalent ratios“I can build equivalent ratios and complete a ratio table.”How can two different-looking ratios describe the same relationship?
Hands-on choice · Build itLearners repeat a group in a tape diagram and see that 6 : 15 is equivalent to 2 : 5.
Practice firstBuild a tape diagram by repeating a group, then fill a practice table with a different ratio.
Hints, from a nudge to a next step
  1. 1Hint 1: Look at each row. How do packs and flashes change together?
  2. 2Hint 2: 4 packs make 6 flashes. What would 2 packs make?
  3. 3Hint 3: 10 packs is 8 packs plus 2 packs. Add those flashes together.
Feedback for a common mistake A learner answers 3 beeps a minute: That’s 18 beeps over 3 minutes. How many in just 1 minute?
The taskFind the unit rate of 6 beeps per minute, then use it: 42 beeps take 7 minutes.
The math6.RP.A.3b · Unit rates“I can find a unit rate and use it to solve a problem.”Why is it useful to know what happens in one unit?
Hands-on choice · Draw itLearners split 20 beeps into equal parts, one for each minute, to find the unit rate.
Practice firstSplit 20 beeps into equal parts to see the unit rate, then use it on a practice buoy.
Hints, from a nudge to a next step
  1. 1Hint 1: 18 beeps over 3 minutes. Split them evenly.
  2. 2Hint 2: Find 1 minute on the number line. How many beeps go with it?
  3. 3Hint 3: Count up by that number until you reach 42.
Feedback for a common mistake A learner answers Plan B: Plan B won’t work. It needs 12 packs, but you have 10. It also takes 8 minutes, and the ship arrives in 7.5.
The taskSet three dials using the logbook clues, test the signal against supplies and time, and explain why it works.
The math6.RP.A.3a–b · Reasoning in context“I can use ratios and rates to choose a plan and explain why it works.”How do ratios and rates help us make a decision?
Hands-on choice · Explain itLearners finish a sentence explaining why a practice signal fits both their supplies and the time.
Practice firstCheck plans against limits, then finish a sentence that explains why a practice signal works.
Hints, from a nudge to a next step
  1. 1Hint 1: Start with the supplies. How many power packs do you have?
  2. 2Hint 2: Is each plan’s time shorter than 7.5 minutes?
  3. 3Hint 3: Use your answers from the generator and radio rooms.

Not covered: plotting ratio pairs (part of 3a), percents (3c) and unit conversions (3d). Learners can also type answers instead of turning the dials.
