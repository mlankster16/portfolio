02 · Background

How I got here

From a backyard telescope to graduate study in how people think and learn, science and learning have always been connected for me, even when the pieces seemed separate. Over time, teaching, curriculum, learning design, and project management have grown into one practice.

Early years1 of 11Looking upChildhood

It started with a telescope in the backyard and a lot of questions about what’s out there. STEM and learning have brought me joy for as long as I can remember.

2005–20092 of 11PhysicsB.S. in Physics, UNC Greensboro

Studied physics, with undergraduate research on stellar spectra and mathematical biology.

2009–20113 of 11Engineering physicsM.S. in Engineering Physics, Appalachian State University

While finishing the degree and running STEM outreach for fun, I started designing and teaching my own courses for gifted 5th–9th graders across North Carolina through Duke TIP. I loved this work so much that it paved the rest of my path for years to come.

2011–20134 of 11Teaching physics and teachersUNC Chapel Hill

Took graduate education courses, led physics labs and recitations as a Teaching Assistant, and designed workshops that helped Durham teachers bring physics into their classrooms.

2012–20145 of 11Science educationM.Ed. in Science Education, NC State University

Focused on digital technologies and curriculum design. The work led to two articles in Science Scope, Science meets engineering (opens in a new tab) and Engineering technology for the digital world (opens in a new tab), a co-authored book chapter, Science teacher attitudes and beliefs (opens in a new tab), and several conference presentations.

2013–20156 of 11A first MOOCUNC Greensboro

In my first instructional design and project management role, I led online courses and programs, including UNCG’s first MOOC, on the soul and the search for meaning. I kept teaching for Duke TIP on weekends, in summers, and online.

2014–20157 of 11Classroom scienceNew Garden Friends School

Taught middle and high school science. Being back in a classroom pulled me further into STEM education research.

2015–20178 of 11Elementary engineeringUNC Greensboro

Helped design mixed-methods research on the Engineering is Elementary curriculum from the Museum of Science, Boston, and designed and led professional development for the teachers using it. The research led to a co-authored paper in the Journal of Research in Science Teaching, “Getting along” and “using evidence” in elementary engineering (opens in a new tab).

2018–20199 of 11Back to ed techHigh Point University

As lead Blackboard administrator, I built bootcamps, workshops, and resources that supported faculty and staff across campus.

2020–now10 of 11STEM learning design at DukeDuke University

Joined Duke as a Learning Experience Designer specializing in STEM, became a Senior Learning Experience Designer a year later, and lead STEM course design and development. Earned the PMP® in 2025.

Next11 of 11What I’m looking forCareer interests

Learning products that give people more ways to explore, practice, and connect what they’re learning to their lives, with AI used thoughtfully.

03 · Teaching and curriculum

Learners I’ve worked with

I’ve taught a range of STEM subjects and learners at different levels. Many of those experiences called for me to develop the curriculum and the learning design together. I see them as closely connected: the curriculum gives us a meaningful destination and the learning design helps learners find their way there.
