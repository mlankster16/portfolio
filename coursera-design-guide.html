Coursera Design Guide

A shared starting point for faculty developing online courses.

I designed and built this website and its companion planning templates for Duke faculty working on Coursera projects. Faculty use them with a Learning Experience Designer to work through the decisions involved in creating an online course.

The guide offers explanations and examples to return to along the way. The templates give faculty and their designer a place to capture their plans.

Where we started

Our planning workbooks combined detailed guidance with spaces to record decisions. As the resources grew, experts and designers could need to search through a lot of material to find what supported the conversation at hand.

I separated those two purposes into connected resources: a website for explanations and examples, and templates for recording a project’s plans. I wanted experts to be able to find useful guidance while keeping their own planning documents focused and manageable.

I developed both resources with support from Claude Design and Claude Code.

Giving each resource a clear purpose

The guide explains what experts are working toward, the questions to consider, and how each decision connects to the learning experience. The templates give experts and their designer a place to record what they decide.

I designed the resources to support the conversations that shape a course. Experts can read an explanation, consider how it applies to their learners and content, and work through the options with their designer.

In the guide · Module, Step 2 of 5

Plan backward from the learning task

What will learners do to show what they’ve learned?

Start here

  1. Decide what learners will do, create, analyze, evaluate, or decide to show they have met the objectives.
  2. Describe what a successful response includes.
  3. Note what learners already know, and where they may struggle.
  4. Identify the explanation, practice, and feedback learners will need before attempting the task.

In the template · Section 2, Plan backward from the learning task

Module learning task
Complete a Data Fitness Review that evaluates whether an available dataset is suitable for addressing a business question and documents concerns that could affect the proposed analysis.
What successful work includes
The review identifies the data the question requires, evaluates the dataset’s relevance and completeness, notes basic quality concerns, and explains how any limitations could affect the analysis.
What learners may need before completing the task
Learners can already recognize common parts of a dataset, including records, fields, and values. They still need criteria for evaluating data in relation to a particular question, and practice explaining why a limitation matters.

Organizing around the work experts are doing

The guide follows the design process from the larger learning goal to courses, modules, and individual learning materials.

Faculty can return to the section that fits their current work, whether they are shaping a course, planning practice, or preparing a video. Guidance on accessibility, copyright, and AI is available along the way.

I wrote the steps as questions so they could support a design conversation. Experts bring deep knowledge of their subject; the questions help us connect that expertise to what learners will do, where they may need support, and how they will practice. An introduction to project roles also makes the collaboration easier to understand from the start.

Specialization

  1. What should learners be able to do by the end of this Specialization?
  2. How should the Courses work together to help learners reach that goal?
  3. What will make this Specialization relevant, discoverable, and appropriately scoped?

Course

  1. What is this Course for, and what should learners be able to do?
  2. What will learners need, and what do we already have or still need?
  3. Which ideas and goals belong together?
  4. What will make this Course relevant, discoverable, and appropriately scoped?

Module

  1. What should learners be able to do by the end of this Module?
  2. What will learners do to show what they’ve learned?
  3. What experiences will prepare learners to complete the task?
  4. Which formats, tools, and resources could support each part of the learner’s experience?

Showing how the decisions connect

I developed one example, Data Analytics for Business Decisions, and carried it through the guide. Experts can follow how an overall goal shapes a course, how the course takes shape through modules, and how a module becomes activities, videos, and assessments.

Using the same example throughout makes those connections visible. Experts can see how a decision carries forward into later planning, and a completed template shows how they might record their own plans.

How the resources are meant to help

I built these resources to help experts prepare for design conversations and continue their planning between meetings. They also give our design team a shared reference for explaining the process and discussing options.

The guide makes the process easier to follow while leaving room for each project’s learners, content, and goals. Experts and their designer use that shared foundation to develop an approach that fits.
