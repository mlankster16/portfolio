Coursera Design Guide

A guide and planning templates for Duke faculty developing online courses and course series on Coursera.

Faculty bring deep expertise to a Coursera project, along with plenty of decisions about designing for online learners.

I built this guide and template set to support those conversations and help our smaller team support more Coursera projects. Faculty use the resources alongside a Learning Experience Designer, with guidance they can return to throughout development.

Faculty don't need to arrive with everything figured out. The guide gives them a starting point for conversations with their designer and a place to return as their ideas develop.

Making the planning tools easier to use

Our planning workbooks combined detailed guidance with spaces to record decisions. That could make it difficult to find the next step or keep the larger learning experience in view. I wanted to make both parts easier to navigate: guidance faculty could explore and templates they could use to plan with their designer.

A place to explore and a place to plan

The guide is a website with a page for each part of the design process. Faculty use it to understand what a decision involves and to bring questions to a design meeting. A set of design templates, one for the Specialization and one for each Course, is where those decisions get written down. The guide has no fields to fill in, and the templates carry very little instruction.

Accessibility, Copyright & AI

The guide moves from the overall learning goal to the experiences that help learners reach it. Faculty can focus on the part they're developing, whether that's planning a course, shaping a module, or creating an activity. Shared guidance on accessibility, copyright, and AI is available throughout.

Questions that guide the conversation

Every page breaks into a few short steps, and each step is framed as a question. A faculty lead can read the question, take it to a design meeting, and leave with a decision to record. Here is one question from the Specialization page, next to the kind of answer it leads to in the worked example.

In the guide: Step 1, Define the goal

What should learners be able to do by the end of this Specialization?

The step suggests describing who the Specialization is for, then drafting 3 to 4 outcomes that say what learners will be able to do.

In the worked example

By the end of the Specialization, learners will be able to frame a business question, analyze and visualize relevant data, and communicate an evidence-informed recommendation.

This goes into Section 1 of the template, along with the title, a short description, and who the Specialization is for.

I also wrote the guide to be clear about who does what. Faculty remain the primary content authors, and the CTL design team helps shape the learning experience and prepares it for Coursera. A short section on the Start Here page lays out both sides, along with the roles on a typical project team.

One example runs through the whole guide

Guidance is easier to follow with something concrete, so I built a single example Specialization, Data Analytics for Business Decisions, and carried it through every page. Faculty can see how a larger goal becomes a Course, how a Course becomes a Module, and how a Module becomes an individual video or quiz. A completed template shows one way to capture that thinking, and a blank version is there for anyone who wants to start fresh.

The example shows how a broad learning goal becomes something learners can practice, with explanations, feedback, and support along the way.
