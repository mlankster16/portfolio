Coursera Design Guide

A guide and template set for Duke faculty designing a Coursera Specialization or Course

Faculty come to a Coursera project with deep expertise and a lot of decisions ahead of them. I built a guide that helps them make those decisions alongside their Learning Experience Designer.

Two workbooks held both the guidance and the fields to fill in

Our team had two planning workbooks for Specialization and Course design. In both, the instructions and the fill-in fields lived together, cell by cell. That made it harder to find the fields to complete and to keep the larger Specialization in view.

I wanted a way to separate the two jobs. One place would explain Coursera and the design process. A different place would hold the decisions faculty and designers record together.

I split the guidance from the template

The guide is a website with a page for each part of the design process. Faculty use it to understand what a decision involves and to bring questions to a design meeting. A set of design templates, one for the Specialization and one for each Course, is where those decisions get written down. The guide has no fields to fill in, and the templates carry very little instruction.

The pages follow the way a project narrows. The Specialization page starts with the larger goal and how Courses fit together. The Course page sets objectives and organizes the learning into Modules. The Module page plans the task, the learning path, and the support learners need. The Learning Assets page covers the videos, readings, activities, and assessments inside each Module. Accessibility, copyright, and AI guidance applies at every level, so it sits on its own page that faculty can return to.

Each page opens with a question to answer

Every page breaks into a few short steps, and each step is framed as a question. On the Specialization page, those are "What should learners be able to do by the end of this Specialization?" and "How should the Courses work together to help learners reach that goal?" A faculty lead can read the question, take it to a design meeting, and leave with a decision to record.

I also wrote the guide to be clear about who does what. Faculty remain the primary content authors, and the CTL design team helps shape the learning experience and prepares it for Coursera. A short section on the Start Here page lays out both sides, along with the roles on a typical project team.

One example runs through the whole guide

Guidance is easier to follow with something concrete, so I built a single example Specialization, Data Analytics for Business Decisions, and carried it through every page. Faculty can see how a larger goal becomes a Course, how a Course becomes a Module, and how a Module becomes an individual video or quiz. A completed template shows one way to capture that thinking, and a blank version is there for anyone who wants to start fresh.

The guide opens by telling faculty they do not need to complete the site or the template before a first meeting. It is there to support the conversation with a designer.
