Project

AI for Nurses

A specialization built with Duke School of Nursing and Johnson & Johnson

Nurses were encountering AI tools in their daily work and needed support understanding what those tools could tell them.

Working with Duke School of Nursing and Johnson & Johnson, I led learning design and project management for a three-course series connecting AI concepts to everyday nursing decisions.

I start by understanding the learners

When I work in a new field, I start by listening. For this project, that meant learning about nurses' working environments, the pressures they face, and what would be useful during a shift. I also worked with our partners to understand their goals and the timeline we needed to work within.

I ask questions before I start building

I started with the survey data already collected from working nurses, using it to understand what they needed to know and be able to do.

From that, I proposed a three-course structure: seeing where AI shows up in nursing practice, evaluating what an AI tool is telling you, and applying that judgment in real clinical reasoning.

From kickoff in September 2025 to launch in August 2026, the work moved from understanding learners' needs through scripts, recording, and interactive design. The full timeline is below.

Learners are involved throughout

During this project, I brought registered nurses into the process repeatedly to test whether our decisions made sense in the context of their actual work.

In one co-design session, nurses described four ways AI was showing up during a shift: something was flagged, something was summarized, a list was reprioritized, or a protocol was triggered. That conversation confirmed we were headed in the right direction and shaped the structure of the courses. The four buckets now appear throughout the series. Here's what those four buckets looked like once we mapped them out for nurses:

Bucket 1

Something is flagged

A score, alert, or Best Practice Advisory fires because the system detected a pattern.

Deterioration alert Fall risk score Sepsis BPA
Bucket 2

Something is summarized

Text is constructed from documented information for a nurse to review.

Discharge summary draft Progress note After-visit summary
Bucket 3

Something is prioritized

A patient list is reordered or ranked by the system.

Reordered patient list Risk-ranked rounding Prioritized inbox
Bucket 4

Something is triggered

A protocol, care plan, or checklist is initiated because a threshold was crossed.

Fall prevention protocol Sepsis bundle Skin care plan

Nurses return to this map throughout all three courses as new vocabulary connects back to a bucket they already recognize.

I'll show you how the pieces fit together

Courses 1 and 2 share a similar structure, built around one question: how do you make technical AI concepts feel relevant to nurses whose focus is patient care?

Each module starts with a subject-matter expert video that introduces the concept in clinical language instead of technical jargon. Then a Term Spotlight takes one term and makes it concrete and relatable, written in the voice of a trusted nurse mentor.

This first activity lets learners compare two kinds of AI side by side: a fall risk score that changes on its own, and a discharge summary that can include an invented sentence. It helps them notice that both can sound confident without showing how they got there.

Try it: predictive vs. generative AI

From there, an Inside the AI Tool interactive connects the concept to tools nurses already use, including MEWS, DAX Copilot, the Epic Sepsis Model, and the Braden Scale. The goal is for nurses to leave each module able to recognize that concept in the systems and workflows they use at work, and not only to define it.

This activity lets learners change the information a model uses and see how its output responds. It gives them a concrete way to explore an abstract idea.

Try it: features vs. labels

We also built in practice assessments as low-stakes checkpoints before the graded work. Each course ends with a Dialogue activity, where nurses bring in a tool from their own practice and work through the course concepts in an AI-powered conversation. Then a graded final assessment confirms they've met the learning goals before the Course Certificate is issued.

In the third course, nurses work through five guided clinical cases. They review the situation, pause during a video to make a decision, and reflect on their reasoning. Each case gives them a chance to apply the ideas from earlier courses.

Case study with pause point

Each activity prepares learners for the next step, moving from concept to tool, to their own practice, to clinical judgment. That progression keeps the learning close to the work nurses do every day.

Accessibility shaped our decisions throughout development, from videos and readings to interactive activities and assessments.
