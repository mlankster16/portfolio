How I view my role

I think of my role as part architect and part translator, and I keep learners at the center of every decision. Most of my projects follow these six steps, and I often circle back to an earlier one when we learn something new. The methods change with each project, depending on who it’s for and where it will live. The same steps have worked for a Coursera Specialization, training for working nurses, and a sixth-grade classroom.

01 · Discover

What’s the goal, and how can learning help?

Why is learning being requested?
An expert wants to reach more peopleLike faculty bringing a course online
People are curiousLearning for its own sake, and for fun
Something isn’t workingPeople are struggling with a task or idea
When something isn’t working, I look at why:
They don’t know howThe idea or process is new to them.Learning design
They haven’t practicedThey know it, but can’t do it well yet.Learning design
They don’t see why it mattersIt feels like extra work without a clear benefit.Learning + support
Something gets in the wayA clunky tool, no time, or an unclear process.Other support
  • I start by listening to stakeholders and subject matter experts, and gathering what already exists.
  • Together, we agree on what success looks like, whether that’s a business result, a credential, or a standard learners need to meet.

What I mean by learning. Learning design helps people build knowledge and skills, explore new ideas, and see why something matters. When a project starts with a problem, it’s worth checking the cause. Sometimes people already know what to do, and something else is in the way, like a clunky tool or a packed schedule. Then learning works best alongside other support, such as a job aid or help from a manager.

03 · Define and map

Where do learners need to end up, and what path gets them there?

  • I start with the end: what learners should be able to do, and how they’ll show it.
  • Then I map the path from the basics to harder skills. The kind of map depends on the project.
Action mapFor job training. Starts with what people need to do at work and what gets in the way, then builds practice around those actions.Example: AI for Nurses
Knowledge graphFor ideas that connect. Shows how concepts build on each other, where they come back, and which misconceptions to plan for.Example: Space Medicine
Curriculum mapFor standards-based units. Lines up goals, lessons, and assessments with the standards learners need to meet.Example: Light the Beacon

04 · Design and prototype

What will learners do, and will it feel real to them?

  • Every experience gets a hook, real practice, and time to reflect. I build practice around realistic decisions, with feedback for the mistakes people actually make.
  • I prototype early, often as a storyboard, a mockup, or a quick scenario, so partners can react to something real.

05 · Test and launch

Does it work for the people it’s for?

  • Experts review scripts and storyboards so the content is right before it’s produced.
  • Then we put drafts in front of learners. I watch how they use them, ask what’s confusing, and revise.
  • Before launch, we check accessibility and quality against the standards that setting uses, and package it for wherever learners will be, from Coursera and Canvas to SCORM and xAPI for other systems.

06 · Evaluate and improve

Did it help, and what would we change?

Four levels I plan for
1. ReactionWas it useful and engaging?Surveys, ratings, comments
2. LearningCan they do it now?Before-and-after checks, assessments
3. BehaviorDo they use it at work or in class?Follow-up, observation, manager or teacher input
4. ResultsDid it make the difference we hoped for?The measure the partner cares about

Some projects come with their own measures of success. When they don’t, I help the team set them before we build, so we’re ready to learn from launch day on. After launch, I look at assessment results, learner feedback, and analytics side by side, and use them to decide where to look closer. We write down what we learned and carry it into the next round.
