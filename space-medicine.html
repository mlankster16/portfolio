Space Medicine

A live 2021 Coursera course I managed and designed, alongside a separate interactive reimagining of Week 2.
Explore the reimagined Week 2 ↓
At a glance

Space Medicine is a Duke course on Coursera with nearly 30,000 learners since 2021. We designed it around what works well on the platform: immersive video, knowledge checks, and reflection. Then, just for fun, I wondered what this successful course could look like if we reimagined it outside of Coursera. Using the course’s existing videos, I rebuilt Week 2 as a guided story with a flight surgeon mentor and a hands-on activity after each video, then built the prototype with Claude Design, Claude Code, and ChatGPT.

01 · The reimagined module

Go for launch

About this prototype: the activity design is my own. The images are concept mockups I generated with ChatGPT for this proof of concept, and I built the interaction with Claude Design and Claude Code.

02 · Key decisions

Three design decisions to support learning

  1. Keep the videos, change what happens around them.Learners watch the same 24 minutes of video as the live course. The new 14 minutes go to mentor chats and five short activities.
  2. Act on each idea right after the video that teaches it.Each video is followed by one hands-on activity, so learners use an idea while it’s fresh, then make the calls in a live scenario on launch day.
  3. Wrap the week in a story with a mentor.Dr. Elena Rivera, a lead flight surgeon, guides learners by chat the night before a high-altitude flight as they investigate the real 1862 balloon flight.

About the live course and my role▾

Space Medicine launched on Coursera in 2021 on a tight timeline. As Project Manager and Learning Experience Designer, I coordinated the media teams and guided the scripts. We built it around what Coursera does well: immersive videos, knowledge checks, and graded quizzes, all accessible, modular, and self-paced.

The live course is unchanged. The reimagined Week 2 is a just-for-fun concept that uses the same videos and content to explore what the week could become on a platform built for interaction.
