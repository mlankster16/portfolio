Branching Biology

Designing for choice in undergraduate science: a shared learning goal with different ways to get there.
Jump to the walkthrough ↓
LearnersUndergraduates in Biology 101, a required science course. The walkthrough follows Jordan, a physics major.
GoalReach the same learning goals through activities shaped by each learner’s interests and prior knowledge
DeliveryA personalized online unit where learners choose among three formats: guided video, escape room and field investigation
Concept RoleSenior Learning and Curriculum Designer
01 · The design

Three ideas behind the design

Learners’ interests and prior knowledge shape the path, while the course’s learning goals stay consistent.
1Meeting learners where they areTheir program, reasons for taking the course and motivation shape the examples and support they see.
2Questions worth investigatingEach unit asks an essential question, drawn from the existing syllabus, that learners investigate with real data.
3Offering learner choiceEach week offers three formats, like a guided video, an escape room or a field investigation, that lead to the same final task.

03 · Behind the concept

Where this concept originated

This proof of concept draws on 13 years of teaching and learning design work in higher education. It takes on tension points that a traditional LMS doesn’t handle well: making a required course feel relevant, offering choice without splitting up assessment, and giving faculty useful signals without over-reading the data.
What I designed and what’s illustrative
Designed and built
  • Essential question and unit goal
  • Three formats with shared checkpoints
  • Feedback, final task, and rubric
  • This interactive walkthrough
Illustrative
  • Learner data and the faculty dashboard
  • Course map results
  • Bird recordings and measurements
  • Platform features, which show how the design could work but aren’t a built product
How I’d validate it
What I’d test first
  • Whether final-task work is comparable across formats, scored blind on the shared rubric
  • Whether checkpoint feedback leads to stronger revisions
  • Whether learners understand how their opening answers are used
  • Whether dashboard signals lead faculty to useful check-ins
What success looks like
  • Similar results across formats
  • Clear gains from the Checkpoint 2 draft to the final task
  • Learners report that the course felt relevant
Working with subject matter experts

Biology faculty would own the content. Before launch, a biology expert would review:

Recordings and dataExplanations offeredKey-terms definitionsRubric language

Claims about urban birdsong are framed here as hypotheses and would be checked against published research and cited recordings.

Accessibility and implementation
Built into the design
  • A spectrogram, a text description of what to notice, and measured values for every recording
  • Captions and transcripts for videos
  • Recording locally is optional
Before it could ship
  • Replace illustrative data with cited recordings
  • Test with screen reader and keyboard users, and with learners who are Deaf or hard of hearing
  • Choose a host platform and plan the connection to the LMS gradebook
  • Set privacy rules for opening-question and dashboard data
