Draft concept

Designing for choice in undergraduate science

A shared learning goal with different ways to get there.

This concept follows Jordan, a physics major taking a required biology course. It explores how their interests and prior knowledge could shape the examples, activities, and support they encounter, while keeping the course’s learning goals consistent.

Proof of concept. I built this from a learning designer’s perspective to show how the learning experience could work. The interface itself would still need UX research and product design.

Jump to the walkthrough

Three ideas behind the design

1

Meeting learners where they are

Learners share their program, why they’re taking the course, and what keeps them going. Their answers shape the examples, preparation, and support they see.

2

Questions worth investigating

The course is organized around essential questions drawn from the existing syllabus and outcomes. Each unit asks a question learners can investigate with real data.

3

Offering learner choice

Each week offers three formats suited to its content. In Week 7, learners investigate through a guided video, an escape room, or a field investigation. Framing from their field carries through each format to the same final task.

The full course mapSee how each week’s question and learner choice carry through the whole course

Every week of the 15-week course asks its own question, built from the existing syllabus, and offers three formats suited to that week’s content. Week 7 is the one Jordan works through below.
